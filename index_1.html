About

My training sits at an unusual junction: a first career in architecture, then a PhD, then a return to spatial cognition and digital modeling. That path shapes how I read a building, a virtual environment, or a piece of neuroscience research — as three descriptions of the same problem.

Most of my current work asks how perception, memory, and culture shape the way people read and inhabit space, physical or virtual, and how that understanding can be encoded into digital twins for design rather than left as intuition.

I write and publish independently, outside institutional affiliation, across journals, edited volumes, and long-form notes.

Frameworks

Neurocultural Architecture

A way of framing spatial design that treats neuro-perceptual response and cultural meaning-making as inseparable inputs, rather than analyzing sensory qualities of a space and its symbolic or cultural reading as two separate exercises.
